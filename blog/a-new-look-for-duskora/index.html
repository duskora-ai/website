A new look for Duskora: redesigned by our own agents

Duskora.ai looks different today. We didn't hire an agency or block out a sprint for it. We wrote a two-line brief, gave it to Duskora running Claude Fable 5.2, and approved the pull request that came back. Here is the old site next to the new one, and the decisions behind the design.

Our old site did its job. It said what Duskora does, showed the app and listed the prices. It was also plain: a light page of cards that could have belonged to anyone. We wanted a site that looks like Duskora and shows the product working, and we wanted to make it the way we ask our customers to make software: by handing the work to agents and keeping the decision to ship.

The brief

This is all we wrote:

Spruce up the website. Keep all the current content and functionality, but make it visually more appealing and add animations: parallax, scroll animations, images that load in different ways, typing text.

We offered to send some sites for inspiration. It didn't need them. The design direction, the motion and every decision below came from Duskora, with Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 as the model behind the agents.

The decisions

A sky at dusk

The name is Duskora, and the logo is a sun going down behind a horizon with three points in orbit around it. The old site used none of that. The new hero is that logo at full size: a night sky, the orbit, and a sun sitting on the horizon. As you scroll, the sun sets behind it. The parallax is there because it is the brand, not because parallax is fashionable.

The hero is dark whether your device is set to light or dark. The app itself is dark, so the site now starts in the same place the product does.

Show the product working

The old hero told you that agents plan, build, review, test and deploy. The new one shows it. The card beside the headline takes an example ticket through each stage, typing out what the agent did, and then it stops. It waits at the approval gate until you press Approve. Only then does it deploy.

That is the most important thing about Duskora, and it is now the first thing you can do on the site: nothing ships without you.

Dark screenshots on a dark page

Screenshots of a dark app looked heavy on a light page. They now sit on a band of night sky in both colour schemes. Each one arrives differently as you scroll to it: wiped in from the side, opened from the centre, wiped up from below, or pulled into focus. Click one and it opens large enough to read.

A roadmap that reads as a timeline

The list of integrations we are working on was seven boxes in a column down the left of the page. It is now a timeline with entries on both sides, and the line down the middle fills in as you read. It takes less room and looks like what it is.

Phones get a menu

On a phone, the old site hid the links to the rest of the page and offered nothing in their place. The new one has a menu button. It is a small thing that the brief never asked for, and the kind of gap you would hope a good engineer notices while they are in the file.

Motion with an off switch

Every animation is optional. If your device asks for reduced motion, nothing moves: no typing, no parallax, no reveals. If JavaScript doesn't load, every word is still on the page, because the text is in the HTML and the script only decides when to show it. The headline that types itself in is one complete sentence to a search engine or a screen reader.

There is no framework and no animation library behind any of it. The site is still plain HTML and CSS with one small script, and it still loads one font.

What didn't change

The brief said to keep the content and the functionality, and that is the part we checked hardest.

  • The words. Headlines, descriptions, prices, plans, the FAQ and the legal pages say what they said before.
  • The behaviour. Currency and billing switches, the beta form, analytics and cookie consent all work as they did.
  • The checks. The site has 290 automated checks, covering everything from prices matching the structured data to no page scrolling sideways on a phone. All 290 passed before the redesign and all 290 pass after it. The agents added 21 more for the new behaviour.

Those checks did real work. They are why the pricing cards still line up, why the page still loads a single font, and why the screenshots still download at the right size for your screen. A redesign is exactly the kind of change that quietly breaks things like that, and here it couldn't.
